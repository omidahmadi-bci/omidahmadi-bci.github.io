Last updated November 2026

I work on keeping EEG signals clean enough to decode in real time, especially on dry-electrode, wearable hardware.

Biomedical engineer with more than ten years of hands-on work on clinical and laboratory devices, and a master’s degree in nuclear engineering with a thesis on real-time adaptive prediction. Since 2025 my focus is brain–computer interfaces (BCI) and EEG devices.

What I work on

Artifact handling on dry electrodes. Dry, low-channel headsets are noisy in ways lab caps are not. I study how automatic artifact rejection and correction change downstream decoding, using reproducible pipelines built on MNE-Python and MOABB.

Latency. A real-time BCI has a hard time budget. I want to know what each preprocessing step costs in milliseconds and what it buys in accuracy.

Small models on limited hardware. Compression and distillation, so a decoder can run on the device that records the signal instead of on a server.

Where I am now. I am building these skills and my first public benchmark. Code will appear on GitHub (opens in a new tab) as soon as it is reproducible.

Earlier research

Real-time adaptive prediction for radiotherapy, under the same constraints that matter in BCI: small data, models fitted per person, and hard latency limits.

Published as Moslem Ahmadi Arbatan.

A synthetic adaptive neuro-fuzzy model for tumour motion tracking

L. Ghorbanzadeh, A. Esmaili Torshabi, J. Soltani Nabipour, M. Ahmadi Arbatan. Technology in Cancer Research & Treatment, 15(2), 334–347, 2016. doi:10.1177/1533034615571153 (opens in a new tab)

Seven neuro-fuzzy models, each built with a different clustering method, were combined into one model that picks the best candidate for each patient and re-selects as treatment goes on. On 20 patients it reduced tracking error against the CyberKnife system’s own model by 49.3% in typical cases and 61.2% in the hardest ones, and it ran faster than the sampling interval.

Link to EEG: per-person model choice under a latency budget, as when a BCI signal drifts between sessions.

Imperialist competitive algorithm for motion dataset optimisation

A. Esmaili Torshabi, M. Ahmadi Arbatan. Iranian Journal of Medical Physics, 18(5), 369–375, 2021. doi:10.22038/IJMP.2020.47283.1752 (opens in a new tab)

An optimisation algorithm chose which of nine input signals the neuro-fuzzy model should use. On the same 20 patients it lowered average error by 36% in regular breathers and 13% in irregular ones, and cut model rebuild time by about 45% and 43%. It did not help every patient, and the paper reports the two cases where it failed.

Link to EEG: fewer, better inputs for a faster model, the logic of channel and feature selection on low-channel headsets.
